\section{Thesis-specific model}\label{sec:thesis-math}

The construction is a deliberately small two-asset implementation of the mean--variance idea in
Markowitz (1952).  Let \(r_t=(r_{S,t},r_{T,t})^{\top}\) denote the adjusted-close returns of SPY
and TLT.  At decision time \(t\), only returns through \(t\) are used.  With at most \(L=126\)
observations, the rolling estimators are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tm-moments}
 \widehat{\mu}_t=252\,\overline r_t,
 \qquad
 \widehat{\Sigma}_t=252\,\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}_t(r)+\epsilon I,
\end{equation}
where the small ridge \(\epsilon I\) makes the numerical covariance positive definite without
materially changing the frontier.

For \(a\in[0.05,0.95]\), define the fully invested portfolio \(w(a)=(a,1-a)^{\top}\).  The
long-only global minimum-variance point and the mean--variance growth anchor are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tm-anchor}
 a_t^{\mathrm{gmv}}=\arg\min_a w(a)^{\top}\widehat\Sigma_t w(a),
 \qquad
 a_t^{\star}=\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal F_t}
 \left\{w(a)^{\top}\widehat\mu_t-\frac{\gamma}{2}
 w(a)^{\top}\widehat\Sigma_t w(a)\right\},
\end{equation}
with \(\gamma=3\).  The set \(\mathcal F_t\) is the estimated efficient segment from the GMV
portfolio to the admissible endpoint having the higher estimated mean.  Because there are two
assets, moving along this segment is equivalent to changing the SPY coordinate \(a\).

The user's proposed ``risk-free'' 60/40 split is instead denoted
\(b=(0.60,0.40)^{\top}\) and treated as a \emph{risky reference portfolio}.  Calling it risk-free
would be mathematically incorrect: its return is stochastic and can be negative.  Over the latest
five completed sessions, define its compound-return gap from the anchor as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tm-growth-gap}
 d_t=\prod_{j=0}^{4}\left(1+w(a_t^{\star})^{\top}r_{t-j}\right)
 -\prod_{j=0}^{4}\left(1+b^{\top}r_{t-j}\right).
\end{equation}
Thus the rule has exactly zero displacement when the two five-session growth rates agree.

Let \(\widehat\sigma_{5,t}\) and \(\widehat\sigma_{21,t}\) be annualised sample volatilities of
\(b^{\top}r\) over five and 21 sessions.  The signed raw frontier coordinate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tm-coordinate}
 q_t=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
 \frac{\widehat\sigma_{5,t}/\widehat\sigma_{21,t}-1}{0.50},-1,1\right)
 \min\!\left\{\frac{|d_t|}{\widehat\sigma_{21,t}\sqrt{5/252}},1\right\}.
\end{equation}
Positive \(q_t\) means the recent volatility estimate has risen and therefore calls for less
risk; negative \(q_t\) calls for more risk.  The absolute growth gap is a throttle, not a directional
return forecast.

For \(n=5\), the causal interpolated coordinate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tm-interpolation}
 \widetilde q_t^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa_j^{(k)}q_{t-n+j},
 \qquad
 \kappa_j^{(k)}=\frac{h_k(j)}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}h_k(\ell)},
\end{equation}
where \(h_{\mathrm{sine}}(j)=\sin(\pi j/(2n))\),
\(h_{\mathrm{linear}}(j)=j\), and \(h_{\mathrm{poly}}(j)=j^2\).  All three filters are one-sided;
none uses a centred window or a future observation.  Finally, with \(a_t^{\mathrm{hi}}\) denoting
the higher-risk endpoint of \(\mathcal F_t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tm-target}
 a_{t+1}=\begin{cases}
 a_t^{\star}+\widetilde q_t(a_t^{\mathrm{gmv}}-a_t^{\star}),
   &\widetilde q_t\geq 0,\\
 a_t^{\star}+(-\widetilde q_t)(a_t^{\mathrm{hi}}-a_t^{\star}),
   &\widetilde q_t<0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The target chosen at the close of \(t\) earns the return from \(t\) to \(t+1\), which is the
no-lookahead convention enforced by the harness.

\section{Trade and broker interpretation}\label{sec:broker-interpretation}

The latest completed-session sine target, dated \LatestModelDate{}, is \LatestModelSPY{} SPY and
\LatestModelTLT{} TLT; its smoothed coordinate is \LatestModelCoordinate{}.  These are model
weights, not a recommendation or an executable order.  Exact share quantities depend on current
IBKR paper-account NAV, positions, FX rates, and prices.  The supplied planner reads those values,
sizes whole-share differences, marks every order \texttt{transmit=False}, and never calls the
broker's placement method.  The full historical target changes are in
\texttt{model\_trade\_ledger.csv}.

Adjusted Yahoo closes are used for the performance study because they represent distributions and
splits.  IBKR \texttt{TRADES} bars are execution prices rather than a total-return series; swapping
them into this study would silently change the return definition.  IBKR is therefore used only for
paper-account contract validation and order planning.
